HOW TO BUILD A PDH PHASOR ANIMATION
====================================

Audience: an AI (or human) that has NOT seen this codebase before, but has
been handed three working pieces of physics code:

  1. A CAVITY object/function that, given a detuning in Hz, returns the
     cavity's complex amplitude reflection coefficient r(detuning) =
     E_reflected / E_incident. (In this repo: OpticalCavity.reflectionCoefficient.)
  2. A REFLECTION SPECTRUM function refl(f) that plots/returns |r(f)|^2
     and/or arg(r(f)) over a swept frequency or detuning axis. (In this
     repo: computeReflectedPowerFrequencyScan.m / plotCavityResponse.m.)
  3. A PDH SPECTRUM function pdh(f) that returns the demodulated PDH error
     signal epsilon(detuning) for a given modulation index, modulation
     frequency, and demodulation phase. (In this repo:
     computePdhErrorSignalDotProduct.m / simulatePdhErrorSignal.m.)

None of these three primitives, by themselves, expose the *phasor* picture
-- the carrier and two sidebands as individual rotating arrows in the
complex plane, whose geometric dot product IS the PDH error signal. That
picture is what this tutorial builds, using the three primitives above as
ingredients and as a correctness check.

Read this end to end before writing code: the pitfalls in Section 6 are
each the result of getting a sign or a scale wrong on the first pass, and
knowing them up front is cheaper than debugging an animation that "looks
plausible but is wrong."

Source material: this project's entire derivation (notation, the phasor
dot-product identity, the timestamps cited throughout this codebase's
comments) follows a single reference webinar --
"Webinar | High-Performance PDH Locking with Reconfigurable
Instrumentation" (Laser 2000 UK / Liquid Instruments, speaker John
Miller, recorded 2024-07-09): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aMtJhPFo9-c
-- specifically its pedagogical walkthrough of the PDH phasor picture
(carrier + counter-rotating sidebands, the dot-product shortcut for the
error signal). Watch or transcript-read that section before implementing
Section 1 below if any step there is unclear from prose alone.


1. THE PHYSICAL PICTURE YOU ARE ANIMATING
------------------------------------------

Phase modulation puts three (relevant) spectral lines onto the laser
field: the carrier (order 0) and two sidebands (order +1, "upper", and
order -1, "lower"), spaced at +/- the modulation frequency f_m. Their
amplitudes come from the Jacobi-Anger expansion:

    a_0  = J_0(beta)
    a_+1 = J_1(beta)
    a_-1 = -J_1(beta)        (Bessel identity J_{-n} = (-1)^n J_n)

where beta is the modulation index. If your cavity/reflection/PDH
primitives don't already expose a0/a1/a-1 as a byproduct, compute them
yourself with the Bessel function of the first kind (besselj(n, beta) in
MATLAB, scipy.special.jv in Python, etc.) -- you do not need any other
part of the modulator model.

Each spectral line reflects off the cavity independently, picking up
whatever complex reflection coefficient the cavity has AT THAT LINE'S OWN
FREQUENCY. At a carrier detuning Delta, the three reflected field phasors
are:

    Z        = a_0  * r(Delta)                     (carrier, reflected)
    E_upper  = a_+1 * r(Delta + f_m)                (upper sideband, reflected)
    E_lower  = a_-1 * r(Delta - f_m)                (lower sideband, reflected)

A photodiode after the cavity sees the beat notes between these lines.
Demodulating at the local-oscillator frequency f_m, referenced to a
demodulation phase theta, picks out the omega_m beat note between the
carrier and each sideband. The two sidebands counter-rotate (this is a
direct consequence of the J_{-1} = -J_1 sign flip), so "freezing" them at
phase theta rotates them by OPPOSITE signs, not the same rotation:

    upperTerm = E_upper * exp(-1i*theta)
    lowerTerm = E_lower * exp(+1i*theta)
    W         = upperTerm + lowerTerm               (the "sideband resultant")

The demodulated PDH error signal is exactly the geometric dot product of
the carrier phasor Z with the sideband resultant W, treating each complex
number as a 2D real vector (Re, Im):

    epsilon = Re(Z) * Re(W) + Im(Z) * Im(W) = Re(Z * conj(W))

This identity is not an approximation or a visual metaphor -- it is a
literal rearrangement of the same algebra your PDH spectrum(f) primitive
already implements. That means you now have two independent ways to
compute epsilon at a given detuning: from your PDH spectrum primitive
directly, or from Z and W above. THEY MUST AGREE, to numerical noise, at
every frame. Use this as your primary correctness check (Section 5).


2. WHAT THE ANIMATION NEEDS TO SHOW
--------------------------------------

A minimal but complete version has two halves, updated together as
detuning Delta sweeps across a span wide enough to see the carrier
resonance and both sideband resonances (a span of about 1.3x f_m on
each side of zero is a reasonable default):

  A. CONTEXT panels (driven by your reflection-spectrum and PDH-spectrum
     primitives, computed ONCE over the full sweep range and never
     recomputed per frame):
       - reflected power |r(f)|^2 vs. detuning, with three vertical
         markers tracking where the carrier and each sideband currently
         sit (at Delta, Delta+f_m, Delta-f_m);
       - the PDH error signal epsilon(f) vs. detuning, with a single
         point marker at the current Delta.

  B. PHASOR panel (recomputed every frame, since it depends on the
     current Delta and theta):
       - arrow for Z (carrier), from the origin;
       - arrow for upperTerm, from the origin;
       - arrow for lowerTerm, drawn TIP-TO-TAIL from the end of
         upperTerm (i.e. starting at upperTerm, not at the origin) --
         this makes their vector sum W visually obvious as the straight
         line from the origin to the second arrow's tip;
       - arrow for the resultant W, from the origin;
       - a thick projection segment of length epsilon along Z's own
         direction (see Section 4 for why this specific construction and
         not "project W onto Z").

Optional but very informative extras, in order of how much they add:
  - live sliders for Delta and theta (turns a fixed sweep into an
    explorable instrument);
  - live sliders for modulation index and both mirror reflectivities, so
    the viewer can move between under/critically/over-coupled regimes
    and under/optimal/over-modulated regimes without restarting;
  - a Play/Stop toggle that auto-advances Delta on a timer, while
    sliders remain live and continuous during playback.


3. INGREDIENT COMPUTATION, PER FRAME
--------------------------------------

Given Delta and theta for the current frame, and a0/a1/am1 computed once
up front (they don't depend on Delta):

    Z         = a0  * cavity.reflectionCoefficient(Delta)
    E_upper   = a1  * cavity.reflectionCoefficient(Delta + f_m)
    E_lower   = am1 * cavity.reflectionCoefficient(Delta - f_m)
    upperTerm = E_upper * exp(-1i*theta)
    lowerTerm = E_lower * exp(+1i*theta)
    W         = upperTerm + lowerTerm
    epsilon   = real(Z)*real(W) + imag(Z)*imag(W)

If your cavity primitive only exposes a "reflection spectrum" function of
absolute frequency rather than a reflectionCoefficient(detuning) method,
adapt the three calls above accordingly -- the math is identical, only
the argument convention changes (absolute frequency vs. detuning from a
given resonance).


4. DRAWING THE DOT PRODUCT ITSELF
-------------------------------------

epsilon is a signed scalar, not a vector, but it's most legible drawn as
a segment of that exact length along Z's own direction:

    if abs(Z) > 0
        unitZ = Z / abs(Z)
    else
        unitZ = 1          % arbitrary; see below for why this is safe
    end
    projectionPoint = epsilon * unitZ
    draw a line from the origin to projectionPoint

Do NOT draw this as "the vector projection of W onto Z" using the formula
|W|*cos(angle) -- that's a different, WRONG quantity: it's missing the
|Z| factor entirely (the true dot product scales with both magnitudes),
and it's undefined when |Z| = 0. Falling back to an arbitrary unitZ (e.g.
1) when Z = 0 is safe precisely because epsilon itself is exactly 0
whenever Z = 0, so the segment collapses to the origin regardless of
which direction you picked -- there's no discontinuity to hide.

A thin dotted connector from the tip of W to projectionPoint helps the
viewer see this as a literal geometric projection rather than an
unrelated overlay.


5. VALIDATION: CROSS-CHECK AGAINST THE GIVEN PDH SPECTRUM PRIMITIVE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before trusting the animation, verify numerically that the frame-by-frame
dot product matches your provided PDH spectrum(f) primitive:

    epsilonFromPhasors  = real(Z)*real(W) + imag(Z)*imag(W)   % per Section 3
    epsilonFromPrimitive = pdhSpectrum(Delta, DemodulationPhase=theta)

    assert(abs(epsilonFromPhasors - epsilonFromPrimitive) < tolerance)

across the full swept range, not just at one detuning. If they disagree:
  - check the counter-rotation SIGNS first (exp(-1i*theta) on upper,
    exp(+1i*theta) on lower, or your codebase's equivalent convention --
    what matters is that they are opposite, not which one is which);
  - check that a_-1 was computed as -J_1(beta), not +J_1(beta);
  - check that your PDH spectrum primitive's own demodulation-phase
    convention matches the theta you're feeding into upperTerm/lowerTerm
    (a sign or 90-degree offset mismatch here is the single most common
    bug in this kind of build).

This cross-check is worth automating as a real unit test if the animation
code is going to live in a codebase with a test suite, not just a manual
eyeball comparison run once.


6. PITFALLS SEEN BUILDING THIS THE FIRST TIME
-------------------------------------------------

- FIXED axis limits, not autoscaled. Compute the largest phasor magnitude
  that can ever occur (roughly (a0 + a1 + abs(am1)) * 1.25) once, up
  front, and set the phasor panel's xlim/ylim to that once. If you let
  the axes autoscale per frame, arrows change apparent length between
  frames even when nothing physically changed, which reads as jitter.

- Use `axis equal` on the phasor panel. Without it, a dot product drawn
  "along Z's direction" will not visually look aligned with Z, because
  the x and y scales differ.

- unwrap(angle(r(f))), not angle(r(f)), when plotting reflected phase
  across a wide span. The far-detuned background reflection sits exactly
  at -r1 (mirror reflectivity), a negative real number -- exactly on the
  +/-180 degree branch cut of angle(). Plotting bare angle() produces a
  visually jarring jump at every point the trace crosses that cut, which
  isn't a real physical feature.

- The near-90-degree angle you'll often see between Z and W away from
  resonance is NOT caused by your choice of demodulation phase theta --
  it's a structural consequence of the sidebands' J_{-1} = -J_1 sign
  flip, present at any theta. Only detuning itself moves this angle
  meaningfully, by breaking the phase relationship between carrier and
  sidebands near an actual resonance. Don't build a slider demo that
  implies "adjust theta to see the angle open and close" -- that's
  showing the wrong knob.

- If you expose a mirror-reflectivity slider, don't put it on a linear
  scale in R. Finesse grows like 1/(1-R), so a linear slider spends
  almost its entire range on Finesse < 100 and crams every interesting
  high-finesse cavity into the last sliver near R=1. Parameterize the
  slider by s = -log10(1-R) ("nines of reflectivity") instead, and invert
  with R = 1 - 10^(-s), so the slider steps through Finesse
  multiplicatively rather than additively.

- Recompute the ingredient sideband amplitudes (a0/a1/am1) and any
  background context curves ONLY when a physics parameter (modulation
  index, mirror reflectivity, modulation frequency) actually changes --
  not on every animation frame. Only Z/upperTerm/lowerTerm/W/epsilon
  need recomputing per frame, since only Delta and theta change during
  a sweep or a play-through.

- When redrawing per frame, update existing graphics objects' data
  (set(handle, "XData", ..., "UData", ...) in MATLAB, or the equivalent
  artist-mutation call in your plotting library) rather than clearing and
  replotting from scratch. Replotting every frame is both slower and
  causes visible flicker.


7. OPTIONAL EXTENSION: THE PHASE-MODULATION PHASOR PICTURE ON ITS OWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

A companion (and simpler) animation, independent of the cavity entirely,
shows carrier + sidebands BEFORE reflection, tracing the "rocking" motion
of a phase-modulated phasor over one modulation period:

    t in [0, 1/f_m], omega = 2*pi*f_m
    upperVec(t)   = a1  * exp(+1i*omega*t)
    lowerVec(t)   = am1 * exp(-1i*omega*t)
    resultant(t)  = a0 + upperVec(t) + lowerVec(t)

Overlay the ideal infinite-order phasor exp(1i*beta*sin(omega*t)) (which
stays exactly on the unit circle for all t) against the truncated
3-line resultant above (which does not, since only the full Jacobi-Anger
sum preserves |E(t)| = 1 exactly). This is a good sanity-building step to
implement BEFORE the full cavity-reflected version in Sections 3-5, since
it exercises the counter-rotation and tip-to-tail drawing mechanics
without also requiring the cavity primitive to be correct.


SUMMARY CHECKLIST
--------------------
[ ] a0, a1, am1 computed from besselj(order, beta), with am1 = -a1
[ ] Z, E_upper, E_lower computed by evaluating the cavity's reflection
    coefficient at Delta, Delta+f_m, Delta-f_m respectively
    (or the equivalent absolute-frequency calls into your primitives)
[ ] upperTerm / lowerTerm rotated by theta with OPPOSITE signs
[ ] W = upperTerm + lowerTerm
[ ] epsilon = Re(Z)*Re(W) + Im(Z)*Im(W), cross-checked against the given
    PDH spectrum(f) primitive across the whole sweep, not just one point
[ ] dot-product segment drawn as epsilon * (Z/abs(Z)), with a safe
    fallback direction when Z = 0
[ ] fixed (not autoscaled) axis limits sized from the max possible
    phasor magnitude
[ ] axis equal on the phasor panel
[ ] unwrap(), not angle(), for any wide-span background phase plot
[ ] per-frame updates mutate existing graphics handles, not replot
