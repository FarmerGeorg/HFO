HOW TO PLOT THE CAVITY REFLECTIVITY'S PHASOR CHAIN
=====================================================

Audience: an AI (or human) that has a working CAVITY implementation and
wants to visualize WHY the reflection coefficient has the value it does,
by drawing it as a chain of individual round-trip contributions added
head-to-tail in the complex plane, rather than just plotting the final
number.

This is a different picture from the PDH carrier/sideband dot-product
phasor animation (see tutorials/pdhphasor.txt) -- that one visualizes how
three ALREADY-REFLECTED spectral lines combine to give an error signal;
this one visualizes how a SINGLE reflection coefficient itself is built
up, term by term, inside one cavity. Read tutorials/reflectedphase.txt
first if the reflection coefficient's phase behavior itself is unfamiliar
-- this tutorial assumes you already know what r(detuning) is and why its
phase does what it does, and focuses purely on how to decompose and draw
it as a chain.


1. THE PHYSICAL PICTURE YOU ARE DRAWING
-------------------------------------------

Light hitting the front mirror splits into two contributions to the
total reflected field:

  - The PROMPT REFLECTION: the part that bounces straight off the front
    mirror without ever entering the cavity. Detuning-independent,
    always exactly -r1 (r1 = sqrt(front-mirror power reflectivity)).

  - The ROUND-TRIP LEAKAGE: light that entered the cavity, circulated
    some number of times, and leaked back out through the front mirror.
    This is an infinite geometric series -- term n is the contribution
    from light that has completed n round trips before escaping:

        term_n = t1^2 * r2 * e^{i*phi} * (r1*r2*e^{i*phi})^n,   n = 0, 1, 2, ...

    where t1 = sqrt(1 - r1^2), r2 = sqrt(back-mirror power reflectivity),
    and phi = 2*pi*detuning/FSR is the round-trip phase.

The reflection coefficient is the prompt term plus the FULL SUM of the
series: r(detuning) = -r1 + sum_n term_n, which closes to the familiar
r(detuning) = (r2*e^{i*phi} - r1) / (1 - r1*r2*e^{i*phi}). What the
"phasor chain" plot shows is the PARTIAL SUMS of that series, drawn as a
connected path -- each partial sum is one more vector added tip-to-tail
onto the last -- so a viewer can watch the sum build up toward the final
closed-form answer rather than just being told the answer.

If your cavity implementation exposes the per-round-trip terms directly
(e.g. a getRoundTripEchoTerms(detuning, numRoundTrips) method returning
an unsummed array), use that. If it only exposes the closed-form
reflectionCoefficient(detuning) and the two mirror reflectivities, derive
the individual terms yourself from the formula above -- you need r1, r2,
and phi(detuning), all of which follow directly from the cavity's own
public properties (mirror power reflectivities and FSR).


2. THE COMPUTATION, STEP BY STEP
-------------------------------------

For one chosen detuning value:

    promptTerm = -r1                                    % scalar, detuning-independent
    echoTerms  = [term_0, term_1, ..., term_{N-1}]       % N-length vector, from Section 1's formula
    chainTips  = [promptTerm, promptTerm + cumsum(echoTerms)]
    points     = [0, chainTips]                          % prepend the origin
    exactValue = cavity.reflectionCoefficient(detuning)  % closed-form cross-check

`points` is now the full sequence of vertices to draw as a connected
path: it starts at the origin, jumps to promptTerm (the prompt-reflection
arrow), then walks through each partial sum of the round-trip series.
`exactValue` should equal `points(end)` to within numerical noise, GIVEN
enough terms N -- see Section 4 for how many is enough. Plot exactValue
as a distinct marker (e.g. an "x") separate from the chain path itself,
so a viewer can see the truncated sum visibly landing on (or falling
short of) the true closed-form point. This cross-check is your primary
correctness signal, same role as the closed-form comparisons in the other
two tutorials in this directory.

Do not forget the origin-prepending step: without it, the plotted path
starts at promptTerm instead of at 0, and the "direct bounce off the
front mirror" segment (0 -> promptTerm) is invisible.


3. CHOOSING WHICH DETUNINGS TO SHOW
-----------------------------------------

Do NOT pick fixed absolute detunings (or fixed absolute round-trip-phase
angles like +/-30 or +/-90 degrees) to illustrate "near resonance" vs.
"off resonance." At high finesse the resonance is an extremely narrow
sliver of one FSR -- at Finesse = 1000, the resonance is only about
360/1000 = 0.36 degrees of round-trip phase wide. Fixed-degree detunings
land far outside it and every one of them will show essentially maximum
reflection, making all your panels look identical and uninformative.

Instead, scale chosen detunings to the cavity's OWN LinewidthFWHM (a
property that already accounts for that cavity's specific finesse):
a good default set, illustrating the full range of interesting behavior,
is:

    0                       (on resonance -- exact cancellation, or as
                             close to it as this cavity's coupling regime
                             allows)
    +/- 0.5 * LinewidthFWHM  (half a linewidth either side)
    +/- 1.5 * LinewidthFWHM  (comfortably outside the resonance dip)

Additionally, detuning = FSR/2 (anti-resonance) is a UNIVERSAL reference
point independent of finesse -- always exactly half an FSR away from any
resonance, where the round-trip leakage rotates all the way around to
point in the SAME direction as the prompt term rather than the opposite
direction, giving this cavity's maximum reflection. Worth including
alongside the linewidth-scaled points precisely because it needs no
finesse-dependent scaling to be meaningful.


4. HOW MANY ROUND-TRIP TERMS TO INCLUDE, AND HOW TO DRAW THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The series converges at a rate set by (r1*r2)^n, i.e. by the same
quantity (g = r1*r2) that sets the cavity's own Finesse -- high finesse
means the light survives many round trips before decaying, which is
exactly why a high-finesse cavity needs far more terms to visually
converge than a low-finesse one:

  - LOW finesse (a handful to a few dozen round trips needed): draw the
    chain with visible markers at each vertex ("-o" style in MATLAB) --
    individual round trips are genuinely countable by eye, and that's
    the pedagogically interesting part for this case.
  - HIGH finesse (thousands of round trips needed, e.g. Finesse = 1000
    needs on the order of numRoundTrips ~ 3000 to converge to graphical
    accuracy): drop the per-vertex markers and draw the partial-sum path
    as a smooth, unmarked curve. At that term count, individual vertices
    are packed far too densely to distinguish, and marker rendering both
    looks cluttered and is needlessly slow.

Whichever term count you choose, ALWAYS verify the closed-form marker
(Section 2's exactValue) visibly lands at the chain's final tip before
trusting the figure -- an under-converged high-finesse chain will visibly
fall short of exactValue, which is your signal to raise numRoundTrips,
not to distrust the closed form.


5. TWO WAYS TO PRESENT IT: ANIMATE, OR LAY OUT AS A GRID
--------------------------------------------------------------

The computation in Section 2 is identical either way; only the outer
presentation differs.

  A. ANIMATED SWEEP: continuously vary detuning across a span (e.g. one
     full FSR, or a fraction of it), recomputing promptTerm/echoTerms/
     chainTips/exactValue each frame and updating existing plot handles'
     XData/YData in place (do not clear and replot from scratch each
     frame -- that's slower and flickers). This is the better choice when
     the point is to SHOW THE TRANSITION: watching the chain visibly
     spiral outward from a near-cancelling collapse on resonance,
     through partial cancellation, out to the anti-resonance point where
     it swings around to reinforce rather than cancel. Use a low-finesse
     cavity for this presentation specifically so the marker-per-vertex
     style from Section 4 stays legible while it's moving.

  B. FIXED SMALL-MULTIPLES GRID: instead of animating, compute the chain
     at a handful of representative detunings (Section 3's list makes a
     good default: 0, +/-0.5 and +/-1.5 linewidths, and FSR/2) and lay
     them out as a tiled grid of static panels (a 2x3 tiledlayout fits
     six panels comfortably), one chain per panel, titled with its own
     detuning label and resulting |r|^2. This is the better choice for a
     report, paper, or any medium where a reader needs to compare several
     cases side by side at their own pace, rather than watch a
     transition play out in real time -- and it's the only sane choice at
     high finesse, where Section 4's thousands-of-round-trips smooth
     curve makes an animated per-frame recompute needlessly expensive for
     no corresponding visual payoff (a smooth spiral doesn't read as
     "spiraling" frame to frame the way a countable low-finesse chain
     does).

Both presentations share every other convention below.


6. SHARED DRAWING CONVENTIONS
-----------------------------------

  - `axis equal` on every panel -- without it, the chain's true geometry
    (angles between the prompt term and the round-trip buildup) is
    visually distorted.
  - FIXED axis limits of [-1.1, 1.1] on both axes, not autoscaled. A
    lossless cavity's reflection coefficient can never leave the unit
    disk (|r| <= 1 always), so this bound is always safe and never
    clips anything -- and fixing it prevents the chain's apparent size
    from jumping between panels/frames for no physical reason (same
    jitter issue called out in tutorials/pdhphasor.txt's pitfalls).
  - Use two distinct colors: one for the prompt-reflection segment
    (origin to promptTerm), a different one for the round-trip buildup
    path (promptTerm's tip through every partial sum). This makes the
    two-term structure of Section 1's formula -- reflection is the
    buildup MINUS the direct bounce, not merely "the sum of everything"
    -- visible directly, rather than requiring the reader to infer it
    from a single uniformly-colored path.
  - Title or annotate each panel/frame with the resulting |exactValue|^2
    -- a numeric readout lets a reader confirm the geometric picture
    (how close the chain's final tip sits to the origin) against the
    actual reflected power, rather than eyeballing distances.


7. THE PHYSICAL STORY TO NARRATE ALONGSIDE THE PLOT
---------------------------------------------------------

Worth stating explicitly (in a caption, title, or accompanying text),
since it's a common and specific misreading of this figure: on
resonance, it is tempting to describe the cancellation as "all the
individual round trips destructively interfere with each other." That is
NOT what the chain shows. The round-trip buildup curve itself always
builds OUTWARD from promptTerm's tip -- the individual round trips
interfere CONSTRUCTIVELY with each other, building up coherently; if they
cancelled among themselves, nothing would build up and there would be no
resonance at all. What actually happens on resonance is a ONE-ON-ONE
cancellation between two SUMS, as seen from the origin: the buildup
curve's final tip lands exactly opposite the tip of the prompt-reflection
arrow, and those two vectors -- not the many individual round trips
composing the blue curve -- are what cancel. Moving off resonance rotates
the buildup's final tip away from that exactly-opposite point, so the
two sums no longer fully cancel and reflected power rises; at the FSR/2
anti-resonance point the buildup has rotated all the way around to point
in the SAME direction as the prompt term, so the two sums add instead of
cancel, giving maximum reflection.


SUMMARY CHECKLIST
--------------------
[ ] promptTerm = -r1, computed once (detuning-independent)
[ ] echoTerms derived either from a dedicated cavity method, or from
    term_n = t1^2 * r2 * e^{i*phi} * (r1*r2*e^{i*phi})^n
[ ] chain vertices = [0, promptTerm, promptTerm+cumsum(echoTerms)],
    origin explicitly prepended
[ ] closed-form exactValue plotted as a distinct marker, verified to
    land at the chain's final tip (raise numRoundTrips if it doesn't)
[ ] chosen detunings scaled to this cavity's own LinewidthFWHM, not
    fixed absolute degrees/Hz, plus the finesse-independent FSR/2
    anti-resonance point
[ ] marker-per-vertex style only for low-finesse (few dozen terms or
    fewer); smooth unmarked curve for high-finesse (thousands of terms)
[ ] axis equal, and fixed [-1.1, 1.1] limits on both axes
[ ] prompt-reflection segment and round-trip-buildup path drawn in two
    distinct colors
[ ] narration/caption states the cancellation as one-on-one between the
    prompt term and the buildup SUM, not a many-way cancellation among
    individual round trips
